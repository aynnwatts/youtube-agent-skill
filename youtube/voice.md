## What I will not claim

That any building, place, ruin or renovation is real. Every video is AI-generated and says so in the
description notice. Narration must never say "we found", "we bought", "our crew" as fact — frame it
as imagined ("Imagine...", "What if...") or as the building's story.

## My format

Target ~10-14 min now (vary it), long-form only, no on-camera presence. Exterior ruin -> exterior
rebuild -> room by room -> reveals. Lean cut: show a repeated process (strip-out, cleaning, sanding)
in full once, compress it in later rooms; each room gets one signature story and an on-screen
heading.

**Audio mix**
- Documentary / room-intro clips: ElevenLabs voice-over + background music.
- Worker restoration clips: NO voice-over, NO music, only realistic work sounds and ambience.
- Reveal clips: background music matched to the reveal's atmosphere, no voice-over.
- Music: YouTube Audio Library tracks under the "YouTube Audio Library License" only (no
  attribution).

## Narration script rules

- Sparse: one unique documentary-style intro per room, silence during the work, a closing
  room-by-room recap, a short CTA.
- Includes an "imagined restoration" line near the start (it is AI-generated; say so).
- Under 10,000 characters per video. New wording every project.
- Mapped to Veo numbers. **One 8 s Veo = one VO line of 12-16 words** (6-7 s spoken, 1-2 s of
  breathing room).
- For every line give: Veo ID, script, target duration, delivery direction, sync cue.
- Keep key words away from the clip edges so a trim does not cut them.
- Never describe an action before it appears on screen.

## Titles and descriptions

- No "101-Year-Old" formula. Honest AI-imagined framing, varied structure.
- Description: AI disclosure near the top, chapters, at most 8 hashtags.
- Data note: the "Abandoned... for 100 Years" / "Forgotten" titles got the most views; the two titles
  that open with "AI-Imagined" got the fewest. Keep the disclosure honest, but it does not have to be
  the first words of the title. The description and the narration can carry it.
